## Interpretation

The selected variant NM_004006.3(DMD):c.2077C>T (p.Gln693Ter) is a nonsense mutation located in a coding exon of the DMD gene. Because it introduces a premature stop codon, it is expected to produce either a truncated dystrophin protein or trigger nonsense-mediated mRNA decay, resulting in loss of functional dystrophin. This loss of function is the primary molecular mechanism that causes Duchenne and Becker muscular dystrophies.  

While the genomic location and ClinVar classification strongly support pathogenicity, additional evidence (functional studies, family segregation, and clinical correlation) would still be required for a definitive diagnosis in a patient.

## Short Reflection

1. What did UCSC show you about your gene that was not obvious from simply reading about the gene’s function?
UCSC revealed that DMD is enormous (~2.22 Mb) with 79 exons separated by extremely long introns, and that it has many alternative transcripts. Reading about its function as a cytoskeletal protein does not convey how large and structurally complex the genomic locus actually is.
2. Why is knowing the exact genomic location of a disease-associated variant useful?
Exact coordinates allow precise mapping to exons/introns/splice sites, enable comparison across databases (UCSC ↔ ClinVar), support primer design or CRISPR targeting, and help determine whether the variant disrupts coding sequence, splicing, or regulatory regions.
3. What is one limitation of predicting a variant’s effect only from its genomic location?
Location alone cannot prove pathogenicity; a coding change may be tolerated, an intron variant may affect splicing only under certain conditions, and functional impact, population frequency, and clinical correlation are still required for a definitive conclusion.
4. What was the most interesting feature you observed about your assigned gene?
DMD is the largest known human gene (~2.2 Mb) with 79 exons, yet most of its sequence consists of huge introns; this extreme size and the presence of many tissue-specific isoforms are striking.
